A move that changes the position, or that speaks a non-empty phrase other than the stop before it (or than the start, before the first stop), is a stop, and the position at each stop is the walk's positions row. A move that does neither is kept: one followed by a move on is an unresolved read, a gap after the stop before it, and is listed on the walk's unresolved row; two in a row are the end only when a move back then reads the stop before the last, not empty, at another position, and a move forward the last again at its position, both on the walk's end row; a failed witness is wrong for good. Every walk is judged whole (judgeWalk): the title, the description, the "Example" heading, and the expected list after it, each in its place; a stop past the last expected one, empty or not, is wrong; a wrong stop always wins, across a gap the next stop must be the next expected one or the one after it, and a walk that ran out of moves is wrong. Only a walk with no wrong stop and a gap, or a stop read as an empty phrase, is walked again, up to three walks, each its own row (`-walk-<n>`), and it never passes. VoiceOver's own help message ("You are currently on ...") is left out only at the position of the stop before it, whose reading speaks the role the help names; it stays in the walk's row, and the web tree spec judges the transcript again from its rows by the same rule, from the positions row.
