- Method: focus the Back button, then `voiceOver.next()` to the end of the screen. VoiceOver's last phrase and the position of its cursor are read over AppleScript after every move: a move that changes the position, or that speaks a different phrase, is a stop; two moves in a row that do neither are the end. Every walk must read the whole screen, each stop in its place (web_tests/vo/s16_vo_walks.js): the title and the description, the "Example" heading, and the expected list after it. A wrong stop, or a walk that reached the end with any expected stop not read, fails the test for good; only a walk cut short before the end with every stop right so far, or one with a stop read as an empty phrase (a failed capture, which never passes) and nothing wrong read, is walked again, up to three walks, each shown as its own row (`-walk-<n>`). VoiceOver's own help message ("You are currently on ...") is not a stop when it is about the element just read: the walk puts the phrase logged for the move in its place and records each stop's cursor position, and the judge leaves out a help message only at the position of the stop before it, whose reading speaks the role the help names (from the reading's role part, never its name); any other is a wrong stop, as is any stop past the end of the screen, an empty one included. No runner retries. VoiceOver's last phrase is text: the voice it was spoken in is not recorded
