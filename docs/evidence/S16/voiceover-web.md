- Method: focus the Back button and capture VoiceOver's last phrase and its cursor's position there (the walk's start row), then `voiceOver.next()` to the end of the screen, capturing both over AppleScript after every move (collectWalk, web_tests/vo/s16_vo_walks.js). A move that changes the position, or that speaks a non-empty phrase other than the stop before it (or than the start, before the first stop), is a stop. A move that does neither is kept: one followed by a move on is an unresolved read, a gap after the stop before it, and is listed on the walk's unresolved row; two in a row are the end only when a move back then reads the stop before the last, not empty, at another position, and a move forward the last again at its position, both on the walk's end row; a failed witness is wrong for good. Every walk is judged whole (judgeWalk): the title, the description, the "Example" heading, and the expected list after it, each in its place; a wrong stop always wins, across a gap the next stop must be the next expected one or the one after it, and a walk that ran out of moves is wrong. Only a walk with no wrong stop and a gap, or a stop read as an empty phrase, is walked again, up to three walks, each its own row (`-walk-<n>`), and it never passes. VoiceOver's own help message ("You are currently on ...") is left out only at the position of the stop before it, whose reading speaks the role the help names. No runner retries. VoiceOver's last phrase is text: the voice it was spoken in is not recorded
