# S06 Screen and page titles

Every screen has a title that says what the screen is for, shown at its
top, and assistive technology is given that title for the screen that is
shown: on the web as the document's title, on Android as the pane title
TalkBack announces, and on iOS as the screen's first heading.

Code: [`lib/techniques/s06_screen_titles/examples.dart`](../../lib/techniques/s06_screen_titles/examples.dart).
Spec: [`docs/specs/S06-screen-titles.md`](../specs/S06-screen-titles.md).
In the app: `/t/S06`, and each example at `/t/S06/<slug>`.

Platforms: Android applies. iOS applies: the title is the screen's level 1
heading, read as a heading when VoiceOver reaches it; when a screen opens
the title is not heard, and VoiceOver's cursor goes to Back
([FL-302](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-302)). Saying the title as a screen
opens is asked only by the Android reference repository, and not of iOS
(the principal's decision, the spec, section 8). The web is a limitation:
Flutter's `Title` sets the document's title when a screen opens, and
nothing sets it back when the screen closes, so after Back the browser
tab, and the page's name in Chromium's accessibility tree, name the screen
that was closed
([FL-300](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-300)). No documented parameter of
`Title`, the app, or the routes changes that, and there is no workaround
in the SDK. By the owner's rule of 2026-11-23, the web therefore has no
good example: the good examples below are right on the web while their
screen is open, and wrong after it closes.

Every example is Flutter's own widgets, configured only through their own
parameters (the owner's rule of 2026-11-21); nothing is built to fill what
they cannot do. No example is a custom solution
([custom-solutions.md](../custom-solutions.md#s06-screen-and-page-titles)).

## Why it matters

A screen's title tells people where they are without reading the screen.
Someone who hears "Orders" as a screen opens knows at once whether the
tap did what they meant; someone moving between browser tabs finds the
one they want by its title. A title that is missing, vague ("Details"),
or left over from the screen before sends them through the content to
find out where they are, or tells them the wrong place.

## WCAG 2.2

- [2.4.2 Page Titled](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#page-titled): "Web pages have titles that describe topic or purpose." As written, a web app at one address can be one page (the glossary's web mail example), and its name can be its title; [Understanding 2.4.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/page-titled.html) says that in a single page application the title "should also be changed dynamically to reflect the content or topic of the current view". This technique reads each screen as a page, as the reference repositories do for native apps, and its verdicts hold only under that reading (the spec, section 3). Its sufficient technique is [G88](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G88), descriptive titles, and its failure [F25](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F25), a title that does not identify the contents.

HTML's own title is the [`title` element](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/title),
which a single-page app changes with `document.title`; Flutter's `Title`
does that on the web. The iOS reference repository's
[Page Titles](https://github.com/cvs-health/ios-swiftui-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/iOSswiftUIa11yTechniques/Documentation/PageTitles.md)
asks for a `.navigationTitle` on each page, read as a heading, and no two
the same. The Android Compose one,
[Screen and Pane Titles](https://github.com/cvs-health/android-compose-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/doc/content/ScreenAndPaneTitles.md),
asks for a visible, descriptive title that screen readers announce, set
as the screen's pane title, and the same for significant panes.

## Good examples

| Example | How | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `good-material` | A button opens a `MaterialPageRoute` whose `Scaffold` has `AppBar(title: Semantics(headingLevel: 1, child: Text('Orders')))`, inside `Title(title: 'Orders')`. `AppBar` makes its title the route's name on Android; `headingLevel: 1` makes it the screen's level 1 heading everywhere (S05); `Title` gives the browser its document title. | [Flutter: AppBar.title](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/AppBar/title.html), [Flutter: Title](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/Title-class.html) |
| `good-cupertino` | A `CupertinoPageRoute` whose `CupertinoNavigationBar` has `middle: Semantics(namesRoute: true, headingLevel: 1, child: Text('Orders'))`, inside the same `Title`. The bar does not make its middle the route's name ([FL-301](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-301)); `namesRoute: true` does. | [Flutter: CupertinoNavigationBar.middle](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/cupertino/CupertinoNavigationBar/middle.html), [Flutter: namesRoute](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/semantics/SemanticsProperties/namesRoute.html) |

What makes them work:

- **The bar's title is the screen's name.** On Android it is the pane
  title of the app's view, which TalkBack announces when the screen opens
  and again for the screen under it when it closes.
- **A `Title` on every screen**, with the same words, for the browser.
  Without one the document keeps the title of the screen before
  (`bad-no-document-title`).
- **A short title.** Material's app bar draws its title on one line and
  cut "Your orders" short at 200% text on a 320 wide screen, in the
  widget tests' font; the iOS reference repository says the same of a
  navigation title.

What they do not get:

- **The web after Back** ([FL-300](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-300)):
  the document keeps "Orders" once the screen has closed.
- **An announcement on iOS** ([FL-302](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-302)):
  when the screen opens VoiceOver reads its Back button, and the title
  only when the cursor reaches it, as "Orders Heading".
- **A Back button without a defect, in `good-cupertino`**
  ([FL-303](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-303)). The bar builds its own
  Back button, which is two buttons, and the one that takes the tap has
  no name. As measured, by platform:
  - iOS: as the screen opens VoiceOver lands on the unnamed one and says
    only "Button", then "Back Button" one move on. This fails 4.1.2's
    name as written.
  - Web: both elements are named "Back" in Chromium's tree, and
    VoiceOver read "Back button" in Playwright's Chromium and WebKit as
    the screen opened (Chromium adding "group"): not a 4.1.2 failure.
  - Android: TalkBack read the node named "Back" as "Back", "Button",
    with no "Double-tap to activate", since that node has no click
    action; an unnamed clickable node is there too, and whether TalkBack
    stops on it was not measured: not shown to fail 4.1.2.

  So `good-cupertino` is the framework's bar through its documented
  parameters, and not an example free of defects.

## Bad examples

Each has one defect, and the tests assert that the defect is present.

| Example | Defect | Fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bad-no-title` | The screen has no title. The `AppBar` has none, there is no `Title`, and "Orders" is drawn as large text, which is plain text: Android's pane title is a space, the browser keeps the example screen's title, and no screen reader finds a heading. | 2.4.2, on all three |
| `bad-vague-title` | The title is "Details", which does not say what the screen is about. | 2.4.2, on all three |
| `bad-no-document-title` | No `Title`: the app bar's title is right, and the browser tab keeps the title of the screen before. On Android and iOS `Title` gives screen readers nothing, so there it has no defect. | 2.4.2, on the web |
| `bad-cupertino-unnamed` | Cupertino's bar as it comes: its title names no route, so on Android the pane title is a space and TalkBack is not told the screen's name ([FL-301](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-301)). | None as 2.4.2 is written: the title is there, shown, and a heading. A framework defect, kept as a bad example (the principal's decision) |

The 2.4.2 verdicts hold under the reading above.

## Example code

Copied from `lib/techniques/s06_screen_titles/examples.dart` by

### Shared code

Used by the examples below.

```dart
/// What every opened screen is about, and its title in the good examples.
const screenTitle = 'Orders';

/// The text of every opened screen.
const screenText = 'Three orders in the last 30 days.';

/// The test identifier of the opened screen's text: `s06-<slug>-text`.
String textId(String slug) => 's06-$slug-text';

/// The opened screen's text, the last thing on it.
class ScreenText extends StatelessWidget {
  const ScreenText({super.key, required this.slug});

  final String slug;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Padding(
      padding: const EdgeInsets.all(16),
      child: Semantics(
        identifier: textId(slug),
        child: Text(screenText, style: Theme.of(context).textTheme.bodyLarge),
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// The example: one button, `s06-<slug>-open`, that pushes the route
/// [route] builds.
class OpenScreen extends StatelessWidget {
  const OpenScreen({super.key, required this.slug, required this.route});

  final String slug;
  final Route<void> Function() route;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Align(
      alignment: AlignmentDirectional.centerStart,
      child: TestId(
        's06-$slug-open',
        child: FilledButton(
          onPressed: () => Navigator.of(context).push(route()),
          child: const Text('Open orders'),
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// The document title the browser shows for a screen titled [title]
/// (`Title`, which `SystemChrome.setApplicationSwitcherDescription` sends
/// on). Its colour must be opaque.
Widget documentTitle(BuildContext context, String title, Widget child) => Title(
  title: title,
  color: Theme.of(context).colorScheme.primary,
  child: child,
);
```

### `good-material`

```dart
/// A Material screen: the `AppBar`'s title names the screen, and `Title`
/// gives the browser the same text. `AppBar` marks its title as the
/// route's name on Android, and as a header; `headingLevel: 1` makes it the
/// screen's level 1 heading on every platform (S05).
class GoodMaterial extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodMaterial({super.key});

  static Route<void> route() => MaterialPageRoute<void>(
    builder: (context) => documentTitle(
      context,
      screenTitle,
      Scaffold(
        appBar: AppBar(
          title: Semantics(headingLevel: 1, child: const Text(screenTitle)),
        ),
        body: const ScreenText(slug: 'good-material'),
      ),
    ),
  );

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) =>
      const OpenScreen(slug: 'good-material', route: route);
}
```

### `good-cupertino`

```dart
/// A Cupertino screen: the `CupertinoNavigationBar`'s middle names the
/// screen. The bar marks it as a header but not as the route's name, so
/// `Semantics(namesRoute: true)` says that it is
/// (docs/flutter-limitations.md, FL-301); `Title` gives the browser the
/// same text.
///
/// Not free of defects: the bar builds its own Back button, two buttons
/// whose tap target has no name (FL-303). On iOS VoiceOver lands on it as
/// the screen opens and says only "Button", which fails 4.1.2's name. On
/// the web both are named "Back" (Chromium), and on Android TalkBack reads
/// the one named "Back"; neither was shown to fail 4.1.2. Nothing in the
/// SDK names it, and the bar's button is kept.
class GoodCupertino extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodCupertino({super.key});

  static Route<void> route() => CupertinoPageRoute<void>(
    builder: (context) => documentTitle(
      context,
      screenTitle,
      CupertinoPageScaffold(
        navigationBar: CupertinoNavigationBar(
          middle: Semantics(
            namesRoute: true,
            headingLevel: 1,
            child: const Text(screenTitle),
          ),
        ),
        child: const SafeArea(child: ScreenText(slug: 'good-cupertino')),
      ),
    ),
  );

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) =>
      const OpenScreen(slug: 'good-cupertino', route: route);
}
```

## What each platform exposes

Measured on Flutter 3.47.5, in the tree tests and the probe
([evidence/probes/s06](../evidence/probes/s06/README.md)); speech from
the probe and the transcripts below.

| | Web | Android | iOS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Where the title goes | `document.title`, from `Title`, and Chromium's name for the page | the pane title of the app's view, from the bar's title | the title's own text, a heading with `headingLevel` |
| `AppBar` title | a heading; a route described by it with `navigator.platform` set to "Linux x86_64", and not as it is on a Mac, in Playwright's Chromium on macOS ([FL-304](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-304)) | the pane title | no route name ([FL-302](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-302)) |
| `CupertinoNavigationBar` middle | a heading; no route name ([FL-301](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-301)) | a pane title of a single space ([FL-301](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-301)) | as `AppBar` |
| When a screen opens | the document takes its `Title`; focus on Back | TalkBack: the pane title, then "Back", "Button" | VoiceOver: "Back Button", or "Button" on a Cupertino screen ([FL-303](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-303)); the title not heard |
| When it closes | the document keeps the closed screen's title ([FL-300](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-300)) | TalkBack: the screen under it, then the button that opened it (once, in a second recording, the app's Back instead) | VoiceOver: the button that opened it |

`bad-no-document-title`'s defect shows on the web only: on Android `Title`
sets the label of the app's task in Recents, which TalkBack does not read
for a screen, and on iOS it does nothing (both read in the source).
`bad-cupertino-unnamed`'s shows on Android only: on iOS no bar's title is
heard when the screen opens, and on the web the document title, which it
has, is the screen's title.

## Flutter limitations

Found on Flutter 3.47.5. Full detail is in the
[limitations log](../flutter-limitations.md).

- [FL-300](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-300): `Title` sets the document title only when it is built or changed, so after Back the browser keeps the closed screen's title.
- [FL-301](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-301): `CupertinoNavigationBar`'s title names no route.
- [FL-302](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-302): on iOS VoiceOver is not told a screen's title when it opens.
- [FL-303](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-303): Cupertino's Back button is two buttons, and the one that takes the tap has no name.
- [FL-304](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-304): on the web a route is a generic element, described by its title only where a node names it.
- [FL-305](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-305): on Android a screen that changes inside one route keeps the pane title of the first.
- [FL-003](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-003), [FL-004](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-004), [FL-005](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-005): why the titles have `headingLevel: 1`.
- [FL-010](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-010): what the web's announcer does when a screen opens.
