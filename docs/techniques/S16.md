# S16 Language of page and parts

The app's language is given to assistive technology, and a passage, a
phrase, or a control's name in another language is marked with its own,
so that a screen reader can speak each in the right voice.

Code: [`lib/techniques/s16_language_page_parts/examples.dart`](../../lib/techniques/s16_language_page_parts/examples.dart).
Spec: [`docs/specs/S16-language-page-parts.md`](../specs/S16-language-page-parts.md).
In the app: `/t/S16`, and each example at `/t/S16/<slug>`.

Platforms: Android and iOS apply; the web is a limitation. Every good
example is Flutter's own documented parameters; none is a custom solution
([custom-solutions.md](../custom-solutions.md#s16-language-of-page-and-parts)).
What Flutter does not give is recorded, not built:

- On the web a phrase inside a sentence cannot be marked: the web engine
  drops `TextSpan(locale:)` and every language attribute of a label
  ([FL-020](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-020)). So the web has no good
  example of a phrase, and `good-phrase` is good on Android and iOS only.
- On the web a locale with a country, `Locale('fr', 'FR')`, is written
  `lang="fr_FR"`, which Chromium and WebKit take as no language
  ([FL-020](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-020), `bad-region-tag`). A locale
  of a language alone is written right.
- `Localizations.override`, documented for a part of an app in another
  locale, sets the whole app's language to its own, and nothing sets it
  back ([FL-360](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-360), `bad-override`).
- A language set inside a node that merges its children, such as a
  `ListTile`'s title, is lost ([FL-361](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-361),
  `bad-title-in-tile`).

What no test here can show is the voice. TalkBack's log gives the language
it asked the speech engine for, and the emulator's engine had no French
voice (its download failed during the probe); VoiceOver's service on iOS and on the web gives words. Where a
language is said here to reach a screen reader, that is what was measured:
a span, an attribute, a `lang`, a logged locale, or a word VoiceOver chose
by it. No voice change was heard.

## Why it matters

A screen reader speaks text with a voice for one language. Text in another
language read with that voice is mispronounced, often past understanding:
French read by an English voice, or a language menu whose every entry is
read as English. A screen reader can change its voice only where the app
says which language the text is in: the app's own language for
everything, and another language for the parts that are in one.

## WCAG 2.2

- [3.1.1 Language of Page](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#language-of-page): the default language of the app, which `MaterialApp` gives the platform: the page's `lang` on the web ([H57](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H57)), the default language of every node on Android and iOS. Every example here is in an app whose language is en-US.
- [3.1.2 Language of Parts](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#language-of-parts): each passage or phrase in another language, marked with its own ([H58](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/html/H58)). The criterion's exceptions (proper names, technical terms, words of indeterminate language, and words that have become part of the surrounding language) do not cover a French sentence or a language's name in that language.

The sources: the iOS reference repository's
[Language](https://github.com/cvs-health/ios-swiftui-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/iOSswiftUIa11yTechniques/Documentation/Language.md)
(a section in another language with its locale, a part of a text with an
attributed string's language) and
[Attributed Strings](https://github.com/cvs-health/ios-swiftui-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/iOSswiftUIa11yTechniques/Documentation/AttributedStrings.md),
the Android View reference repository's
[Text Language Identification](https://github.com/cvs-health/android-view-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/doc/componenttypes/TextLanguageIdentification.md)
(a `LocaleSpan` over a part of a text, and the app's language for 3.1.1),
and HTML's [`lang`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Global_attributes/lang).

## Good examples

| Example | How | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `good-passage` | A French passage after an English line, inside `Semantics(localeForSubtree: Locale('fr'))`. The web: `lang="fr"` on the passage's element; Android: a `LocaleSpan` fr over its text, for which TalkBack logged the locale `fr` in the probe; iOS: the element's language, by the engine's source, and VoiceOver's words the same as unmarked. | [Flutter: Semantics.localeForSubtree](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/widgets/Semantics/localeForSubtree.html), iOS Language, H58 |
| `good-phrase` | A French phrase inside an English sentence, a `TextSpan(locale: Locale('fr'))` in a `Text.rich`. Android: a `LocaleSpan` fr over the phrase, and TalkBack speaks the sentence in three parts, the phrase in French; iOS: VoiceOver reads it with a second space round the phrase. **The web: nothing** ([FL-020](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-020)). | [Flutter: TextSpan.locale](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/painting/TextSpan/locale.html), Android View Text Language Identification |
| `good-language-menu` | A language menu, each language named in its own: each `ListTile` inside `Semantics(localeForSubtree:)` with its language. Each tile is a button in its language on every platform; VoiceOver on iOS reads "Français Bouton". | iOS Language, H58 |

What makes each of them work:

- **A locale of a language alone**, `Locale('fr')`. With a country it is
  written `fr_FR` on the web, which the browser takes as no language.
- **`localeForSubtree` round the node, not inside it.** Inside a node that
  merges its children, such as a `ListTile` or `TestId`, it is lost
  (FL-361).
- **`Semantics(localeForSubtree:)`, not `Localizations.override`**, which
  sets the whole app's language (FL-360).
- **The app's language from `MaterialApp`.** This app sets no locale and
  keeps the default `supportedLocales`, en-US, the language of its text.
  An app in another language gives `MaterialApp` that locale (the probe
  built one in French: the page's `lang` was `fr`).

## Bad examples

Each has one defect, and the tests assert that the defect is present.

| Example | Defect | Fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bad-unmarked` | The French passage is not marked: it is in the app's language, English | 3.1.2, on every platform |
| `bad-title-in-tile` | The language is set on each tile's title, inside the tile, and is lost ([FL-361](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-361)): each language is in English | 3.1.2, on every platform |
| `bad-region-tag` | `localeForSubtree: Locale('fr', 'FR')`: on the web `lang="fr_FR"`, which the browser takes as no language ([FL-020](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-020)) | 3.1.2 on the web; none on Android and iOS |
| `bad-override` | `Localizations.override` round the passage: the whole app becomes French, and stays French after the screen closes ([FL-360](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-360)) | 3.1.1 on Android and the web, measured; on iOS by the engine's source |

`bad-override` changes the app it runs in: once it has been opened, the
catalog app is French until it is started again. The tests open each
example in a fresh start of the app.

### Shared code

Used by the examples below.

```dart
/// French, by language alone. A locale with a country, `Locale('fr', 'FR')`,
/// is written `fr_FR` on the web, which no browser reads as French
/// (`bad-region-tag`, FL-020).
const french = Locale('fr');

/// The English line before a passage, in the app's own language.
const lead = 'The sign at the door reads:';

/// The passage in French.
const passage = 'Bienvenue à tous nos visiteurs.';

/// The languages of the language menus, each named in its own language,
/// with its name in English for the "Chosen" line.
const languages = [
  (code: 'fr', name: 'Français', english: 'French'),
  (code: 'es', name: 'Español', english: 'Spanish'),
  (code: 'de', name: 'Deutsch', english: 'German'),
];

/// The line under a language menu that says which language was chosen
/// last: "Chosen: French". [id] is its test identifier.
class Chosen extends StatelessWidget {
  const Chosen({super.key, required this.id, required this.chosen});

  final String id;
  final String chosen;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Padding(
      padding: const EdgeInsets.only(top: 8),
      child: Semantics(
        key: ValueKey<String>(id),
        identifier: id,
        liveRegion: true,
        child: Text('Chosen: $chosen'),
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// One language of a language menu.
typedef Language = ({String code, String name, String english});

/// A language menu: one `ListTile` per language, titled by [title], each
/// tile with its test identifier inside [around], and the "Chosen" line
/// under them.
class LanguageMenu extends StatefulWidget {
  const LanguageMenu({
    super.key,
    required this.slug,
    required this.title,
    this.around = _itself,
  });

  final String slug;
  final Widget Function(Language language) title;
  final Widget Function(Language language, Widget tile) around;

  static Widget _itself(Language language, Widget tile) => tile;

  @override
  State<LanguageMenu> createState() => _LanguageMenuState();
}

class _LanguageMenuState extends State<LanguageMenu> {
  var _chosen = 'none';

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Column(
      crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.stretch,
      children: [
        for (final l in languages)
          widget.around(
            l,
            TestId(
              's16-${widget.slug}-${l.code}',
              child: ListTile(
                title: widget.title(l),
                onTap: () => setState(() => _chosen = l.english),
              ),
            ),
          ),
        Chosen(id: 's16-${widget.slug}-chosen', chosen: _chosen),
      ],
    );
  }
}
```

### `good-passage`

```dart
/// A passage in French after a line in English: the passage inside
/// `Semantics(localeForSubtree: Locale('fr'))`. Its node is French, and
/// the English line keeps the app's language. Web: `lang="fr"` on the
/// passage's element. Android: a `LocaleSpan` fr over its text. iOS: the
/// element's language is fr (source read; XCUITest cannot read it).
class GoodPassage extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodPassage({super.key});

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Column(
      crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.start,
      children: [
        const Text(lead),
        const SizedBox(height: 8),
        Semantics(localeForSubtree: french, child: const Text(passage)),
      ],
    );
  }
}
```

### `good-phrase`

```dart
/// A phrase in French inside a sentence in English: `TextSpan(locale:)`
/// round the phrase, whose documentation says it sets how assistive
/// technology pronounces the text. Android: a `LocaleSpan` fr over the
/// phrase alone. iOS: the phrase's range of the label carries the language
/// (source read). Web: nothing; the web engine drops a part's language,
/// so the phrase is not marked there (FL-020), and the web has no good
/// example of a phrase.
class GoodPhrase extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodPhrase({super.key});

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return const Text.rich(
      TextSpan(
        text: 'The guide said ',
        children: [
          TextSpan(text: 'bon voyage', locale: french),
          TextSpan(text: ' as we left.'),
        ],
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

### `good-language-menu`

```dart
/// A language menu, each language named in its own language: each
/// `ListTile`, with its test identifier, inside
/// `Semantics(localeForSubtree:)` with its language, so that the tile's own
/// node, a button, is in that language on every platform. The language is set round the whole tile: set inside it, on
/// its title, it is lost (`bad-title-in-tile`).
class GoodLanguageMenu extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodLanguageMenu({super.key});

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return LanguageMenu(
      slug: 'good-language-menu',
      title: (l) => Text(l.name),
      around: (l, tile) =>
          Semantics(localeForSubtree: Locale(l.code), child: tile),
    );
  }
}
```

## What each platform exposes

Measured on Flutter 3.47.5. The tree is from the tree tests and the probe
([evidence/probes/s16](../evidence/probes/s16/README.md)). Web: the page's
`lang` attributes and the browser's own `:lang()` matching, in
Playwright's Chromium and WebKit; Chromium's accessibility tree holds no
language. Android: the Android 17 emulator, the spans on each node's
description and TalkBack's log. iOS: the iOS 27.0 simulator; XCUITest
cannot read an element's language.

| | Web | Android | iOS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The app's language | the page's `lang="en-US"` | a `LocaleSpan` en-US over every node's text; TalkBack logs `en_US` | every element's language, by the engine's source (not readable) |
| `localeForSubtree: Locale('fr')` (`good-passage`) | `lang="fr"` on the node's element | a `LocaleSpan` fr over the node's text; TalkBack logged `fr` (probe) | by the engine's source; a button inside it is read with its role word in French ("Français Bouton", `good-language-menu`) |
| `TextSpan(locale:)` round a phrase (`good-phrase`) | nothing | a `LocaleSpan` fr over the phrase, beside the app's over the whole; TalkBack speaks three parts, logging `en_US`, `fr`, `en_US` | VoiceOver reads a second space before and after the phrase |
| `Locale('fr', 'FR')` (`bad-region-tag`) | `lang="fr_FR"`, matched as no language | a `LocaleSpan` fr-FR | by the engine's source, fr-FR |
| `localeForSubtree` inside a `ListTile` (`bad-title-in-tile`) | no `lang` | the app's `LocaleSpan` en-US | "Français Button" |
| `Localizations.override` (`bad-override`) | the page's `lang="fr"`, and still after Back | a `LocaleSpan` fr over every node, and still after Back | by the engine's source |

## Flutter limitations

Found on Flutter 3.47.5. Full detail is in the
[limitations log](../flutter-limitations.md).

- [FL-020](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-020): on the web a part's language is dropped, and `localeForSubtree` with a country or a script writes a `lang` that is no language tag.
- [FL-360](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-360): `Localizations.override` sets the whole app's language, and nothing sets it back.
- [FL-361](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-361): a language set inside a node that merges its children is lost.
- [FL-434](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-434): Material's focus tint on a `ListTile`, under 3:1 against the page, in `good-language-menu` as in S08's tiles.
