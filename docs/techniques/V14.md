# V14 Focus indicators

Someone using a keyboard can see which control has focus: the control
changes visibly when focus reaches it, the change stands out at 3:1 or
more from what is next to it, and nothing drawn over the screen hides the
focused control.

Code: [`lib/techniques/v14_focus_indicators/examples.dart`](../../lib/techniques/v14_focus_indicators/examples.dart).
Spec: [`docs/specs/V14-focus-indicators.md`](../specs/V14-focus-indicators.md).
In the app: `/t/V14`, and each example at `/t/V14/<slug>`.

Which controls take focus is [I01](I01.md), the order of Tab is
[I02](I02.md), and where focus goes when the screen changes is
[I03](I03.md). The contrast of text is for the other visual techniques
(V01 Colour contrast, V02 High contrast preference, V03 Dark and light
themes); this one measures the focus indicator only.

## Custom solutions

**Custom solution: not vetted by a real-world example.** `good-material` and
`good-widgets` are custom solutions. Each is tested here in isolation, with
screen-reader transcripts, but has not been built into a real app and tested
in a user journey. Do not treat them as vetted techniques.

What is built by hand:

- `good-material`: A focus ring drawn by hand round the CheckboxListTile,
  from a Focus listener and a DecoratedBox, as the tile has no style for one
  (FL-430).
- `good-widgets`: A card drawn by hand, with focus, keys, and a focus ring
  built from FocusableActionDetector.

The register of custom solutions, with the examples judged not to be one, is
[custom-solutions.md](../custom-solutions.md#v14-focus-indicators).

## Why it matters

A keyboard user, a switch user, or anyone who moves through a screen one
control at a time needs to see where they are before they press Enter.
Flutter draws every focus indicator itself, on every platform, so what
the user sees is whatever the app's widgets paint, and Flutter's defaults
are faint. Most Material controls show focus by tinting their own
background, or the page round them, a tenth of the way towards the text
colour, which is easy to miss; a checkbox, radio button, or switch adds a
pale disc round itself at about 1.2 to 1 against the page, and darkens a
border or a thumb that was already dark; a checked checkbox shows the
disc alone. Cupertino controls draw a pale ring that is about 1.9 to 1 on
a light page. Someone with low vision may not find any of these. And when a banner or a bar is drawn over
a scrolling list, Tab can land on a control that is underneath it.

## WCAG 2.2

- [2.4.7 Focus Visible](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-visible): a keyboard-operable interface has a mode in which the focus indicator is visible. Flutter's rule, showing most indicators only after a key and hiding them after a touch, is such a mode ([Understanding 2.4.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html)). An indicator removed altogether is not ([F78](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F78)).
- [2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#focus-not-obscured-minimum): the control with focus is not entirely hidden by content the app draws, such as a sticky footer ([Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html), [F110](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F110)). Partly hidden passes.
- [1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#non-text-contrast): the indicator is visual information that identifies a state, so it needs 3:1 against the colours next to it: the page, for one drawn outside the control; the colours on both sides, for one inside it or on its edge ([Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). A change of the control's own background is out of its scope, and the criterion does not compare the focused and unfocused states: a border that darkens from one dark grey to another meets it if the darker grey is 3:1 against what is next to it. The exception for an appearance "determined by the user agent" does not cover Flutter's defaults: on the web Flutter hides the browser's outline and paints its own, and on Android and iOS Flutter's widgets are part of the app, not the platform's controls ([WCAG2ICT on 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/#non-text-contrast); the spec, section 3, gives the reasoning).

2.4.13 Focus Appearance is AAA and not claimed; the good examples' rings
would meet its change of 3:1, and Flutter's default tints and halos, and
a checkbox border going a shade darker (1.82 to 1), would not.

```dart
/// Lays out one example and says which control was used last, so people
/// and tests can tell which control a key acted on.
class _Demo extends StatefulWidget {
  const _Demo({required this.slug, required this.builder});

  final String slug;
  final List<Widget> Function(BuildContext context, ValueChanged<String> used)
  builder;

  @override
  State<_Demo> createState() => _DemoState();
}

class _DemoState extends State<_Demo> {
  String _last = 'none';

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Column(
      crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.start,
      spacing: 16,
      children: [
        ...widget.builder(context, (name) => setState(() => _last = name)),
        Semantics(
          key: ValueKey<String>('v14-${widget.slug}-count'),
          identifier: 'v14-${widget.slug}-count',
          child: Text('Last used: $_last'),
        ),
      ],
    );
  }
}

const _alerts = 'Weather alerts';
const _save = 'Save';

/// A focus ring for a Material button: a 3 pixel line of the theme's
/// `onSurface` colour drawn just outside the button's shape while it has
/// focus, and nothing otherwise. It comes from the focused state, so it is
/// drawn whatever moved focus there, not only after a key. Being outside
/// the button, the ring lies on the page, and `onSurface` is the colour the
/// theme puts text in on that page.
ButtonStyle _ringed(ColorScheme colors) => ButtonStyle(
  side: WidgetStateProperty.resolveWith(
    (states) => states.contains(WidgetState.focused)
        ? BorderSide(
            color: colors.onSurface,
            width: 3,
            strokeAlign: BorderSide.strokeAlignOutside,
          )
        : null,
  ),
);

/// Draws the same ring round [child] while focus is on it or inside it.
/// The `Focus` here takes no focus itself; it only hears when the control
/// inside it gains or loses focus.
class _FocusRing extends StatefulWidget {
  const _FocusRing({required this.child});

  final Widget child;

  @override
  State<_FocusRing> createState() => _FocusRingState();
}

class _FocusRingState extends State<_FocusRing> {
  bool _focused = false;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final colors = Theme.of(context).colorScheme;
    return Focus(
      canRequestFocus: false,
      skipTraversal: true,
      includeSemantics: false,
      onFocusChange: (value) => setState(() => _focused = value),
      child: DecoratedBox(
        position: DecorationPosition.foreground,
        decoration: BoxDecoration(
          borderRadius: BorderRadius.circular(8),
          border: Border.all(
            color: _focused ? colors.onSurface : Colors.transparent,
            width: 3,
          ),
        ),
        child: widget.child,
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// The trails of the scrolling examples.
const _trails = [
  'Ridge trail',
  'Lake loop',
  'Summit path',
  'Forest walk',
  'River run',
  'Canyon climb',
];

/// Six trail buttons in a scrolling box 232 pixels tall, and a notice 72
/// pixels tall. With [over], the notice is drawn over the bottom of the
/// box, as a banner pinned to the bottom of a view often is; otherwise it
/// sits under the box, and the box is that much shorter.
class _TrailList extends StatelessWidget {
  const _TrailList({
    required this.slug,
    required this.over,
    required this.used,
  });

  final String slug;
  final bool over;
  final ValueChanged<String> used;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final colors = Theme.of(context).colorScheme;
    final list = SingleChildScrollView(
      child: Column(
        crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.stretch,
        children: [
          for (final (i, trail) in _trails.indexed)
            Padding(
              padding: const EdgeInsets.symmetric(horizontal: 8, vertical: 4),
              child: SizedBox(
                height: 48,
                child: TestId(
                  'v14-$slug-trail${i + 1}',
                  child: TextButton(
                    style: _ringed(colors),
                    onPressed: () => used(trail),
                    child: Text(trail),
                  ),
                ),
              ),
            ),
        ],
      ),
    );
    final notice = Semantics(
      container: true,
      identifier: 'v14-$slug-notice',
      child: Container(
        height: 72,
        width: double.infinity,
        alignment: Alignment.center,
        padding: const EdgeInsets.symmetric(horizontal: 12),
        color: colors.inverseSurface,
        child: Text(
          'Trail conditions are updated every morning.',
          style: TextStyle(color: colors.onInverseSurface),
        ),
      ),
    );
    return Semantics(
      container: true,
      identifier: 'v14-$slug-list',
      child: Container(
        height: 232,
        decoration: BoxDecoration(border: Border.all(color: colors.outline)),
        child: over
            ? Stack(
                children: [
                  list,
                  Positioned(left: 0, right: 0, bottom: 0, child: notice),
                ],
              )
            : Column(
                children: [
                  Expanded(child: list),
                  notice,
                ],
              ),
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

```dart
/// A `CheckboxListTile` and a `FilledButton` whose focus is shown by a ring
/// of the theme's text colour. The button draws it from its style, for the
/// focused state; the tile, which has no such style, gets it from a
/// `Focus` that hears its focus change.
class GoodMaterial extends StatefulWidget {
  const GoodMaterial({super.key});

  @override
  State<GoodMaterial> createState() => _GoodMaterialState();
}

class _GoodMaterialState extends State<GoodMaterial> {
  bool _on = false;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final colors = Theme.of(context).colorScheme;
    return _Demo(
      slug: 'good-material',
      builder: (context, used) => [
        TestId(
          'v14-good-material-alerts',
          child: _FocusRing(
            child: CheckboxListTile(
              controlAffinity: ListTileControlAffinity.leading,
              contentPadding: const EdgeInsets.symmetric(horizontal: 8),
              value: _on,
              onChanged: (value) {
                setState(() => _on = value!);
                used(_alerts);
              },
              title: const Text(_alerts),
            ),
          ),
        ),
        Padding(
          padding: const EdgeInsets.all(4),
          child: TestId(
            'v14-good-material-save',
            child: FilledButton(
              style: _ringed(colors),
              onPressed: () => used(_save),
              child: const Text(_save),
            ),
          ),
        ),
      ],
    );
  }
}
```

```dart
/// A card drawn by hand. `FocusableActionDetector` gives it focus, Enter
/// and Space, and tells it when to show its focus: only when the keyboard
/// is in use, as Flutter's own controls do. The card then draws a border 3
/// pixels wide in the theme's text colour over its edge.
class GoodWidgets extends StatefulWidget {
  const GoodWidgets({super.key});

  @override
  State<GoodWidgets> createState() => _GoodWidgetsState();
}

class _GoodWidgetsState extends State<GoodWidgets> {
  bool _showFocus = false;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final colors = Theme.of(context).colorScheme;
    return _Demo(
      slug: 'good-widgets',
      builder: (context, used) {
        Object? activate(Intent _) {
          used(_trails.first);
          return null;
        }

        return [
          TestId(
            'v14-good-widgets-trail',
            child: FocusableActionDetector(
              mouseCursor: SystemMouseCursors.click,
              actions: {
                ActivateIntent: CallbackAction<ActivateIntent>(
                  onInvoke: activate,
                ),
                ButtonActivateIntent: CallbackAction<ButtonActivateIntent>(
                  onInvoke: activate,
                ),
              },
              onShowFocusHighlight: (value) =>
                  setState(() => _showFocus = value),
              child: Semantics(
                button: true,
                child: GestureDetector(
                  onTap: () => used(_trails.first),
                  child: Container(
                    constraints: const BoxConstraints(
                      minHeight: 56,
                      minWidth: 220,
                    ),
                    padding: const EdgeInsets.symmetric(
                      horizontal: 16,
                      vertical: 12,
                    ),
                    decoration: BoxDecoration(
                      color: colors.surfaceContainerHighest,
                      borderRadius: BorderRadius.circular(12),
                      border: Border.all(color: colors.outline),
                    ),
                    // Drawn over the card's edge, so that the card's size
                    // and its contents stay where they are.
                    foregroundDecoration: _showFocus
                        ? BoxDecoration(
                            borderRadius: BorderRadius.circular(12),
                            border: Border.all(
                              color: colors.onSurface,
                              width: 3,
                            ),
                          )
                        : null,
                    child: Row(
                      mainAxisSize: MainAxisSize.min,
                      spacing: 12,
                      children: [
                        Icon(
                          Icons.map_outlined,
                          color: colors.onSurfaceVariant,
                        ),
                        Flexible(
                          child: Text(
                            _trails.first,
                            style: TextStyle(color: colors.onSurface),
                          ),
                        ),
                      ],
                    ),
                  ),
                ),
              ),
            ),
          ),
        ];
      },
    );
  }
}
```

## What each platform exposes

Measured on Flutter 3.47.5, light mode on the devices. Every colour is
read from screenshots; the ratios are against the page, `#FBF8FF`.

| | Web | Android | iOS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who draws the indicator | Flutter, on the canvas; the browser's outline is on a transparent element and never seen | Flutter | Flutter |
| Default Material tint (buttons, tiles) | 1.15:1 to 1.32:1 against what it replaced | the same, with keys from a registered keyboard; none with `input keyevent` ([FL-433](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-433)) | as on the web |
| Checked checkbox's halo, all that changes | 1.15:1 | 1.15:1 | 1.15:1 |
| `CupertinoButton.filled`'s ring, in this app, in light mode | 2.49:1 | 2.49:1 | 2.49:1 |
| Good examples' rings | 16.30:1, 19.97:1 | 16.30:1, 19.97:1 | 16.30:1, 19.97:1 |
| Keys from | Playwright | a USB keyboard the test registers with `uinput` | XCUITest's hardware keys |
| `bad-notice-over-list`, share of each focused trail in sight | 1, 1, 0.88, 0, 0, 0 | 1, 1, 0.87, 0, 0, 0 | 1, 1, 0.88, 0, 0, 0 |
| Focus stop read from | `document.activeElement` | `isFocused` | what Space then does |
| Screenshots from | Playwright | `UiAutomation.takeScreenshot` | `XCUIScreen`, drawn into sRGB |
| Screen reader | the controls' names and roles; nothing about focus indicators | the same | the same |

The widget tests give the same colours as the web and iOS, to the last
digit, in light and dark mode; the dark mode figures are in the spec.

Things to know:

- **Do not rely on the defaults.** Where a Material control has nothing
  of its own to change (a checked checkbox, a `TextButton`, an
  `IconButton`, a `ListTile`), its tint is all there is, under 3:1 on the
  page ([FL-430](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-430)). Elsewhere it meets
  1.4.11 by a border, ring, or thumb that was dark already, which is hard
  to notice ([FL-434](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-434)). Cupertino's
  ring is under 3:1 on a light page, and a `CupertinoCheckbox`'s on a
  dark one too ([FL-431](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-431)).
- **A ring of the text colour is the simplest fix.** For a Material
  button, `ButtonStyle.side` for the focused state; for anything else, a
  `Focus` that hears the focus change and a border; for Cupertino,
  `focusColor`. Draw it outside the control so it lies on the page and
  only has to stand out from the page.
- **Never make the overlay transparent without putting something in its
  place.** On a Material button it is the only focus indicator.
- **Flutter shows most indicators only after a key.** A touch hides them,
  with focus kept, and the next key brings them back
  (`FocusManager.highlightMode`). That meets 2.4.7. Indicators drawn from
  the focused state itself, such as `ButtonStyle.side` and a `Focus`
  ring, show whenever the control has focus.
- **On Android, keys from the virtual keyboard device do not turn
  highlights on** ([FL-433](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-433)), and the
  keys `input keyevent` and Instrumentation send are all from it. A test
  that presses keys that way sees what someone using an on-screen
  keyboard's keys would, not a hardware keyboard's user. The V14 Android
  test registers a USB keyboard with the shell's `uinput` tool, whose
  keys turn highlights on as a real keyboard's do.
- **Do not draw a bar or banner over a scrolling view.** Flutter scrolls a
  focused control into the view and does not know what covers it
  ([FL-432](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-432)). Lay the bar out beside the
  view, or make a header a pinned sliver of the same `CustomScrollView`,
  which Flutter does allow for. `Scaffold(extendBody: true)` with a bottom
  bar has the same problem.

## Flutter limitations

Found on Flutter 3.47.5. Full detail is in the
[limitations log](../flutter-limitations.md).

- [FL-430](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-430): a checked `Checkbox`, a `TextButton`, an `IconButton`, and a `ListTile` show focus only with a tint under 3:1 on the page. `good-material` adds a ring.
- [FL-431](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-431): Cupertino's focus ring is under 3:1 on a light page: the button's, the switch's, and the checkbox's, which is also under 3:1 in dark mode. `good-cupertino` sets `focusColor`.
- [FL-432](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-432): a focused control is scrolled into its view without regard to what is drawn over it. `good-notice-below` lays the notice out beside the view.
- [FL-433](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-433): on Android a key from the virtual keyboard device does not turn highlights on, and `input keyevent` and Instrumentation's keys all come from it; a keyboard registered through `uinput` does turn them on.
- [FL-434](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-434): elsewhere Material's default focus indicator is a faint tint or halo beside a part that does meet 3:1, or a tint of the control's container; not a WCAG failure as written.
- [FL-435](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-435): a `CupertinoTextField` shows focus only by its caret.
- [FL-035](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-035): a checkbox is a switch to XCUITest and VoiceOver on iOS, which the iOS tests expect.

## How it is tested

| Layer | Where | Run |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Widget tests | `test/techniques/v14_focus_indicators_test.dart` | `flutter test` |
| Web tree | `web_tests/v14_focus_indicators.spec.js` | `flutter build web`, then `npx playwright test v14_focus_indicators.spec.js` in `web_tests/` |
| Android tree | `android/app/src/androidTest/.../V14FocusIndicatorsTest.kt` | `python3 tool/evidence.py V14 --only android` |
| TalkBack speech | same test class, `talkBackWalk` | `python3 tool/talkback.py V14` |
| iOS tree | `ios/RunnerUITests/V14FocusIndicatorsUITests.swift` | `python3 tool/evidence.py V14 --only ios` |
| VoiceOver speech (iOS) | same test class, `testVoiceOverSpeech` | `python3 tool/voiceover_ios.py V14` |
| VoiceOver speech (web) | `web_tests/vo/v14_focus_indicators_vo.spec.js` | `python3 tool/voiceover_web.py V14`; needs Guidepup setup, takes over the screen |

On Android the keys come from a USB keyboard the test registers with the
shell's `uinput` tool for each test and removes after it, so that they
arrive as a hardware keyboard's (FL-433); the FL-433 test also presses Tab
with `input keyevent` to show the difference. It runs at the emulator's
own size and at CI's 320 by 640 at 160 dpi.

Focus visibility is measured from pixels on every layer, the same way:
the control's rectangle is read, the screen is captured before Tab
reaches the control and twice after, once the screen has stopped
changing, and the control must not have moved. Every pixel round the
control that changed in both captures and agrees is part of the
indicator, whatever it replaced. It is split into bands (connected areas
of one colour, a one pixel line among them; the blended edges of shapes
are left out), and each band is taken against what is next to it in the
focused capture, from every pixel of its edge: a band drawn where the page
was, against the page; one drawn on the control, against every colour on
either side of it, the least contrasting deciding. The colours and ratios
come from those pixels, never from the theme. A pixel that changes in one
capture only (a caret, an animation) does not count, and a change in
which the colours only swapped places (a label or an icon that moved)
fails the test. A good control needs about a line of pixels at 3:1 or
more (a size of the tests' own: 1.4.11 sets none); a check that nothing
is drawn first measures the Back button's indicator through the same
steps in the same launch; and when Tab moves on, the control's area must
be as it was before, pixel for pixel (the last good control of each
example; every good control on iOS). The measure is a test of these
examples, not a general check of conformance, and the spec lists what it
is known not to do. It has its own cases, in one file that every layer
runs (`test/techniques/v14_focus_indicators_measure_cases.json`), on
made-up pictures: Understanding 1.4.11's own pass and fail figures, a
border round near-identical shades and round a checkerboard, gradients,
one pixel lines at pixel ratios 1 to 3, a moved shape, a blinking caret,
and the known limits. The 2.4.11 tests read, at each Tab stop, how much
of the focused trail lies inside the scrolling box and outside the
notice.

[VoiceOver on web](../evidence/V14/voiceover-web.md).
