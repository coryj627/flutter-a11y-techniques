# C18 Card

A card's parts are exposed as what they are: its heading as a heading on
its own, its text as text, its picture with a text alternative, and each
action as a control whose name says what it acts on. A card that is one
control is a button named by its text, and keeps that role. A link in a
card says where it goes by itself, not by a heading that nothing relates
to it.

Code: [`lib/techniques/c18_card/examples.dart`](../../lib/techniques/c18_card/examples.dart).
Spec: [`docs/specs/C18-card.md`](../specs/C18-card.md).
In the app: `/t/C18`, and each example at `/t/C18/<slug>`.

Platforms: Android, iOS, and the web apply. Every good example is
Flutter's own widgets through their documented parameters; none is a
custom solution ([custom-solutions.md](../custom-solutions.md#c18-card)).
What Flutter does not give a card is recorded, not built: no platform is
told that a card is a card ([FL-026](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-026)),
and a heading inside a control makes it a heading and not a button on the
web ([FL-262](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-262)), so a card that is one
control has no heading of its own here.

## Why it matters

A card gathers one subject: a trail, a product, an offer. Someone who sees
it takes in its title, its text, its picture, and what they can do with it
at a glance, and knows where one card ends and the next begins. A screen
reader user gets that only from the parts' roles and order: the title read
as a heading that they can jump to, the text read as text after it, and
actions whose names say what they act on, because "Save" heard on its own,
in a list of cards, could be any of them.

Flutter's `Card` gets this wrong by default: it joins all its text into one
node, so the whole card is read as its heading.

## WCAG 2.2

- [1.3.1 Info and Relationships](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#info-and-relationships): the heading is exposed as a heading and the text as text, as they are shown.
- [2.4.4 Link Purpose (In Context)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#link-purpose-in-context): a link in a card says where it goes alone or with context related to it. A heading before a link is not such context in HTML (the [Understanding document](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html) lists H80, a link with the heading before it, as advisory only), and [F63](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F63) fails a link whose context is only in content not related to it.

The sources: the iOS reference repository's
[Cards](https://github.com/cvs-health/ios-swiftui-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/iOSswiftUIa11yTechniques/Documentation/Cards.md)
(a heading first, and actions "unique and specific when spoken"), and
HTML's [`article`](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/article)
(each one identified by a heading). The Android reference repositories have
no card entry.

## Good examples

| Example | How | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `good-material` | `Card(semanticContainer: false)`, as the `Card` documentation says for a card with different kinds of content, round a title with `Semantics(headingLevel: 3)`, the text, `Image(semanticLabel:)`, and two `TextButton`s named "Save Ridge trail" and "Share Ridge trail" with `Text(semanticsLabel:)`. Each part is read on its own, in order. | [Flutter: Card.semanticContainer](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/Card/semanticContainer.html), iOS Cards |
| `good-tappable` | A `ListTile(onTap:, title:, subtitle:)` in a `Card`: one button, named by the title and the text. Its title is not a heading (FL-262). | iOS Cards (a card combined into one element) |
| `good-cupertino` | Cupertino has no card: a `DecoratedBox` in Cupertino's grouped background, with the same heading, text, and picture as `good-material`, and no action (a `CupertinoButton` needs an `Actions` built by hand for Enter on the web, [FL-002](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-002)). | MDN article |

What makes each of them work:

- **`semanticContainer: false`** on a `Card` with more than one kind of
  content. Left at its default, the card's texts join one node, and the
  heading's level is on it ([FL-269](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-269),
  `bad-default-container`).
- **No heading inside a control.** A `ListTile` merges its title and
  subtitle into its node, and a heading level there makes the node a
  heading on the web, with no button role
  ([FL-262](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-262), `bad-heading-button`).
- **A `ListTile`, not an `InkWell`**, for a card that is one control. The
  `Card` documentation's tappable card is an `InkWell`, which has no button
  role on Android and iOS ([FL-401](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-401),
  `bad-ink-well`).
- **Names that say what an action acts on.** "Save" and "Share" are
  visible; `semanticsLabel` adds the trail, starting with the visible word
  (2.5.3), as the iOS reference asks with `.accessibilityLabel`.
- **A focus outline** from the buttons' own `style`: Material's focus tint
  on a `TextButton` is under 3:1 ([FL-430](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-430)).
  `good-tappable`'s focus is the tile's tint inside the card, 1.32:1 in
  light and 1.44:1 in dark, a change of background within the component
  that 1.4.11 as written leaves out ([FL-434](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-434)).

## Bad examples

Each has one defect, and the tests assert that the defect is present.

| Example | Defect | Fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bad-default-container` | `Card` at its default: the heading and the text are one node, a level 3 heading, which holds the button ([FL-269](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-269)) | 1.3.1, on every platform |
| `bad-heading-button` | `good-tappable` with its title a heading: on the web an `<h3>` with no button role, which a DOM click does not activate ([FL-262](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-262)); on Android and iOS a heading and a button | 4.1.2 on the web; none on Android and iOS |
| `bad-ink-well` | A `Card` round an `InkWell`, the `Card` documentation's tappable card: no button role on Android and iOS ([FL-401](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-401)) | 4.1.2 on Android and iOS; none on the web, where any tappable node is a button |
| `bad-read-more` | A link named "Read more" under the heading, which nothing relates to it (C03's link, as Flutter has no link widget) | 2.4.4 (F63), on every platform |

### Shared code

Used by the examples below.

```dart
/// The card's subject, the same in every example.
const trailName = 'Ridge trail';

/// The card's text.
const trailText = 'A 6 km climb with a view.';

/// The picture's text alternative.
const pictureLabel = 'Sunrise over the valley';

/// The card's heading: level 3, under the screen's level 1 title and its
/// level 2 "Example". `headingLevel` makes it a heading on every platform
/// (FL-003, FL-005) and the right level on the web (FL-004).
class CardHeading extends StatelessWidget {
  const CardHeading({super.key});

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Semantics(
      headingLevel: 3,
      child: Text(trailName, style: Theme.of(context).textTheme.titleLarge),
    );
  }
}

/// The card's picture, with a text alternative (S02).
Widget picture() => Image.memory(
  sunrisePng,
  width: 96,
  height: 64,
  fit: BoxFit.cover,
  filterQuality: FilterQuality.none,
  semanticLabel: pictureLabel,
);

/// A 2 pixel outline in the page's text colour while a `TextButton` has
/// keyboard focus, through the button's own `style`, as in C12: Material's
/// own focus tint on a `TextButton` is under 3:1 against the page
/// (docs/flutter-limitations.md, FL-430).
ButtonStyle focusRing(ColorScheme colors) => ButtonStyle(
  side: WidgetStateProperty.resolveWith(
    (states) => states.contains(WidgetState.focused)
        ? BorderSide(color: colors.onSurface, width: 2)
        : null,
  ),
);

/// A count under a card, so that people and tests can confirm which action
/// ran: "Saves: 1". [id] is its test identifier.
class Count extends StatelessWidget {
  const Count({super.key, required this.id, required this.text});

  final String id;
  final String text;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Semantics(
      key: ValueKey<String>(id),
      identifier: id,
      liveRegion: true,
      child: Text(text),
    );
  }
}

/// Lays out one example: the card and its counts below it. [builder] gets
/// a function that adds one to a count by its name.
class CardDemo extends StatefulWidget {
  const CardDemo({
    super.key,
    required this.slug,
    required this.counts,
    required this.builder,
  });

  final String slug;

  /// The names of the counts, in order: "Saves", "Shares", "Opens".
  final List<String> counts;
  final Widget Function(BuildContext context, void Function(String) add)
  builder;

  @override
  State<CardDemo> createState() => _CardDemoState();
}

class _CardDemoState extends State<CardDemo> {
  final _counts = <String, int>{};

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Column(
      crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.stretch,
      spacing: 8,
      children: [
        widget.builder(
          context,
          (name) => setState(() => _counts[name] = (_counts[name] ?? 0) + 1),
        ),
        for (final name in widget.counts)
          Count(
            id: 'c18-${widget.slug}-${name.toLowerCase()}',
            text: '$name: ${_counts[name] ?? 0}',
          ),
      ],
    );
  }
}
```

### `good-material`

```dart
/// A card with separate parts: a heading, its text, a picture, and two
/// actions. `semanticContainer: false` keeps each part a node of its own,
/// as the `Card` documentation says for a card that "contains multiple
/// different types of content". Each button's name says which trail it
/// acts on, with `Text(semanticsLabel:)`, and starts with its visible word.
class GoodMaterial extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodMaterial({super.key});

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final colors = Theme.of(context).colorScheme;
    return CardDemo(
      slug: 'good-material',
      counts: const ['Saves', 'Shares'],
      builder: (context, add) => Card(
        semanticContainer: false,
        child: Padding(
          padding: const EdgeInsets.all(16),
          child: Column(
            crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.start,
            spacing: 8,
            children: [
              const CardHeading(),
              const Text(trailText),
              picture(),
              Wrap(
                spacing: 8,
                children: [
                  TestId(
                    'c18-good-material-save',
                    child: TextButton(
                      style: focusRing(colors),
                      onPressed: () => add('Saves'),
                      child: const Text(
                        'Save',
                        semanticsLabel: 'Save $trailName',
                      ),
                    ),
                  ),
                  TestId(
                    'c18-good-material-share',
                    child: TextButton(
                      style: focusRing(colors),
                      onPressed: () => add('Shares'),
                      child: const Text(
                        'Share',
                        semanticsLabel: 'Share $trailName',
                      ),
                    ),
                  ),
                ],
              ),
            ],
          ),
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

### `good-tappable`

```dart
/// A card that is one control: a `ListTile` with `onTap`, which is a button
/// on every platform, named by its title and its text. Its title is not a
/// heading: a heading merged into a control is a heading and no longer a
/// button on the web (`bad-heading-button`).
class GoodTappable extends StatelessWidget {

  @override

      builder: (context, add) => Card(
        clipBehavior: Clip.antiAlias,
        child: TestId(

          child: ListTile(
            title: const Text(trailName),
            subtitle: const Text(trailText),
            onTap: () => add('Opens'),
          ),
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

### `good-cupertino`

```dart
/// Cupertino has no card. Its grouped background and a rounded box draw
/// one; a box gives assistive technology nothing, so the parts are nodes
/// of their own, as in `good-material`: a heading, its text, a picture.
class GoodCupertino extends StatelessWidget {
  const GoodCupertino({super.key});

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final text = CupertinoTheme.of(context).textTheme;
    return DecoratedBox(
      decoration: BoxDecoration(
        color: CupertinoColors.secondarySystemGroupedBackground.resolveFrom(
          context,
        ),
        borderRadius: BorderRadius.circular(12),
      ),
      child: Padding(
        padding: const EdgeInsets.all(16),
        child: Column(
          crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.start,
          spacing: 8,
          children: [
            Semantics(
              headingLevel: 3,
              child: Text(trailName, style: text.navTitleTextStyle),
            ),
            Text(trailText, style: text.textStyle),
            picture(),
          ],
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}
```

## What each platform exposes

Measured on Flutter 3.47.5. Screen reader speech is from the transcripts
below; the rest from the tree tests and the probe
([evidence/probes/c18](../evidence/probes/c18/README.md)).

| | Web | Android | iOS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A card with separate parts (`good-material`) | a level 3 heading, the text, an image, two buttons, as siblings; no element for the card | a heading `View`, the text's `View`, an `ImageView`, two `Button`s | the heading (type other), the text, an image, two buttons |
| Screen reader on its parts | "heading level 3 Ridge trail", the text, "Sunrise over the valley image", "Save Ridge trail button" (Chromium and WebKit) | "Ridge trail / Heading", the text, "Sunrise over the valley / Image", "Save Ridge trail / Button" | "Ridge trail Heading", the text, "Sunrise over the valley Image", "Save Ridge trail Button" |
| A card that is one control (`good-tappable`) | a button named by the title and the text | a `Button` named by both | a button named by both |
| `Card` at its default (`bad-default-container`) | an `<h3>` named by the title and the text, holding the button; VoiceOver's next went from the heading to the count and did not stop on the button, which Tab reaches | one heading `View` named by both, holding the button | one element named by both, read with "Heading" |
| Heading inside a control (`bad-heading-button`) | an `<h3>`, no button role; a DOM click does nothing | a `Button` that is a heading | a button, read with "Button Heading" |
| `InkWell` card (`bad-ink-well`) | a button | a clickable `View`, no role | static text, no role |
| "Read more" link (`bad-read-more`) | `<a href>` "Read more", outside the heading | a `View` "Read more" with its `URLSpan` | a link "Read more" |
| Tab | Back, then each control | the same | the same (two Tabs reach the first control) |

Things to know:

- **No platform is told where a card begins and ends.** Flutter has no
  `article` role ([FL-026](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-026)), and a `Card`
  with `semanticContainer: false` is no node at all on the web and Android
  (iOS's hierarchy was not read). Its heading is what marks it. Cards in a
  `ListView` are each an item of a list on Android (TalkBack: "In list. 3
  items"), and a group with no role on the web; on iOS the probe saw no
  difference (probe fixture p14).
- **A control inside a tappable card is reached on its own** on Android and
  the web (probe fixtures p07 and p08: TalkBack focus and Tab reach both,
  and each runs its own action), and on iOS, where VoiceOver stops on both.
  On the web it is a button inside a button (p08) or inside the card's
  `<h3>` (p07); ARIA makes a button's children presentational, so no
  example builds it.
- **No good example is a link.** A Flutter link is built by hand
  ([FL-221](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-221)), so 2.4.4's passing case, a
  link in a card that names its subject or a card that is one link, has no
  good example here (the principal's decision). `bad-read-more`'s link is
  C03's custom build; what it shows is the name "Read more", not the link.
- **`TestId` goes round controls, never round a card.** It is a
  `MergeSemantics`, and round a card with one button (probe fixture p18)
  it makes one node named by the whole card, with the heading's level and
  the button's action: on Android a button that is a heading, on iOS a
  button (VoiceOver's reading was cut at 64 characters before any trait),
  and on the web a heading only.
- **The iOS reference's combined card** (one element, its actions added
  back with `.accessibilityAction`) is not built: Flutter's custom actions
  are not exposed on the web ([FL-022](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-022)).

## Flutter limitations

Found on Flutter 3.47.5. Full detail is in the
[limitations log](../flutter-limitations.md).

- [FL-262](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-262): on the web a node with a heading level is a heading and nothing else; a control merged with a heading loses its role.
- [FL-269](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-269): a `Card` at its default joins all its text into one node, and a heading in it makes all of that one heading.
- [FL-401](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-401): an `InkWell` with `onTap` is not a button on Android and iOS; the `Card` documentation's tappable card is one.
- [FL-026](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-026): no `article` role; what a card and a list of cards give each platform instead.
- [FL-430](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-430), [FL-434](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-434): Material's focus tint is faint on a `TextButton` and inside a card.
- [FL-002](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-002): Enter does not activate a `CupertinoButton` on the web, which is why `good-cupertino` has no action.
- [FL-006](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-006): any tappable node is a button on the web.
- [FL-022](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-022): custom semantics actions are not exposed on the web.
