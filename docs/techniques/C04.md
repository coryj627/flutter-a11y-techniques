# C04 Links inline with text

Each link in a sentence is a stop of its own, with the link role where the
platform has one and a name that, with its sentence, says where it goes. It
is read in its place in the sentence, is reached and followed by touch, by
the keyboard, and by a screen reader, and is told from the words round it
by more than colour.

Code: [`lib/techniques/c04_links_inline_with_text/examples.dart`](../../lib/techniques/c04_links_inline_with_text/examples.dart).
Spec: [`docs/specs/C04-links-inline-with-text.md`](../specs/C04-links-inline-with-text.md).
In the app: `/t/C04`, and each example at `/t/C04/<slug>`.

Platforms: Android, iOS, and the web apply. On iOS see
[FL-246](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-246): on the iPhone 17 screen the
good sentences take two lines, and the node of their last words covers
both lines, over the links, so a VoiceOver user who explores by touch and
puts a finger on a link hears "first." and not the link. Swiping, the
keyboard, and the double tap reach and follow every link. No workaround was
found, and no WCAG failure is claimed.

A link on its own is [C03 Stand-alone link](C03.md), which this builds on.
How big a link must be is V13; a link in a sentence is exempt from the
target size minimum.

## Why it matters

A link in a sentence has to work three ways at once. A screen reader user
hears the sentence and the link in it, in order, and can tell the link is
one. A keyboard user can Tab to it. And someone who cannot tell colours
apart can still see which words are the link.

Flutter has no link widget, and its own way of putting a link in text, a
`TextSpan` with a tap recognizer, is read well but cannot be reached from
the keyboard. A link that can be reached is a widget inside the text, a
`WidgetSpan`, and that brings three traps of its own: it is read out of its
sentence unless told otherwise, it is scaled twice at large text sizes, and
it cannot break across lines with the words round it.

## WCAG 2.2

- [2.4.4 Link Purpose (In Context)](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#link-purpose-in-context): the link's name, alone or with its programmatically determined link context, says where it goes. The sentence round a link is that context ([G53](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G53)) where the platform relates it to the link: on the web and Android a Flutter sentence's words and links share one parent, in order with FL-413's workaround; on iOS VoiceOver reads them in order (the hierarchy was not read). Every link here says where it goes by its own text, so no example fails 2.4.4.
- [1.4.1 Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color): a link in text is told from the words round it by more than colour. An underline does it. A colour alone passes only with a contrast of 3:1 or more against the words ([G183](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G183)); a colour with less fails ([F73](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F73)), and a cue that comes only on hover or focus does not count (F73).

| `good-custom` | `FocusableActionDetector` round `Semantics(link: true, linkUrl:)` and a `GestureDetector`: the widgets layer only. | APG: Link |

What makes each of them work, and is in `LinkSentence` and the two link
widgets:

- **A `WidgetSpan`, not a `TextSpan`.** A widget in the text can take
  keyboard focus; a span cannot ([FL-240](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-240),
  `bad-text-span`).
- **FL-413's two-part workaround.** A `semanticsIdentifier` on the first
  words makes the paragraph give each run of words a node of its own, with
  sort keys 0, 1, 2; each link gets a sort key between the runs round it,
  0.5 and 1.5. Without the identifier the sentence is one label with the
  links left out, read before them (`bad-out-of-order`); without the sort
  keys the order rests on where the links happen to land
  ([FL-413](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-413)).
- **The link's text in `MediaQuery.withNoTextScaling`.** A `WidgetSpan`
  scales its widget by the text size setting as a whole, so a `Text` in it
  is scaled twice: at 200% the link would be drawn at 400%
  ([FL-241](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-241)).
- **`linkUrl`, and the browser left to follow it on the web**, as in C03
  ([FL-032](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-032),
  [FL-013](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-013)), and `onTapHint` for TalkBack
  ([FL-220](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-220)).
- **An underline**, and a 2 pixel focus ring drawn outside the link, so
  that it covers none of its letters.

## Bad examples

Each has one defect, and the tests assert that the defect is present.

| Example | Defect |
| --- | --- |
| `bad-text-span` | The link is a `TextSpan` with a `TapGestureRecognizer`, the SDK's own link in text. It is read in its place, and a tap works, but a keyboard cannot reach it ([FL-240](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-240)). It opens a page that nothing else opens. |
| `bad-out-of-order` | The links are `WidgetSpan`s with no workaround: a screen reader reads "Read about and first." and then the links ([FL-413](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-413)). |
| `bad-colour-only` | No underline, and the words' own weight and style: only colour tells the links apart, and the theme's primary is 2.66:1 against the words in light mode and 1.32:1 in dark (measured by the widget test), under F73's 3:1. |
| `bad-merged` | `MergeSemantics` round the sentence, which `TestId` also is: the sentence and both links are one link, with the first link's address and action ([FL-243](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-243)). |

What each fails, by platform, as the criteria are written:

| Example | Android | iOS | Web |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `bad-text-span` | 2.1.1: Tab never reaches the link, and no key opens its page, which nothing else opens | 2.1.1: no key opened its page | 2.1.1: Tab never reaches it, and no key opens its page |
| `bad-out-of-order` | 1.3.2: the sentence node is read "Read about and first.", its links after it | 1.3.2: as on Android, with VoiceOver | 1.3.2: a group named "Read about and first." holding the links |
| `bad-colour-only` | 1.4.1 (F73) | 1.4.1 (F73) | 1.4.1 (F73) |
| `bad-merged` | 4.1.2: the second link is not exposed as a link of its own; TalkBack's double tap follows the first | 4.1.2: the second link is not exposed as a link of its own; VoiceOver reads one link | 4.1.2: one `<a href="#/t/C04">`; a click on either link's words or on the first words, and Enter, follow the first link |

```dart
/// One link in a sentence: its text, and the app's page it goes to.
class InlineLink {
  const InlineLink(this.text, this.route);

  final String text;
  final String route;

  /// The link's address, for `Semantics(linkUrl:)`. On the web it is the
  /// `href` of the link's `<a>`, and the app keeps its route after the `#`.
  Uri get address => Uri.parse('#$route');
}

/// The two links of the sentence. Each name says where it goes: this
/// technique's page, and C03's.
const inTextLink = InlineLink('links in text', '/t/C04');
const standAloneLink = InlineLink('stand-alone links', '/t/C03');

/// What a tap does. Off the web the app follows the link. On the web the
/// browser follows the link's `href` itself, so the app must not follow it
/// as well (docs/flutter-limitations.md, FL-013; C03's
/// `bad-app-also-follows`).
void followLink(BuildContext context, InlineLink link) {
  if (!kIsWeb) Navigator.of(context).pushNamed(link.route);
}

/// The look of link text in [color]: underlined, so that it is told from
/// the text round it by more than colour (WCAG 1.4.1).
TextStyle inlineLinkStyle(Color color, {bool underline = true}) => TextStyle(
  color: color,
  decoration: underline ? TextDecoration.underline : TextDecoration.none,
  decorationColor: color,
);

/// A 2 pixel ring in the colour of the page's text round [child] while
/// focus is on it or inside it. It is drawn outside the link's box, so that
/// it does not cover the link's letters. The `Focus` here takes no focus
/// itself; it only hears when the link inside it gains or loses focus.
class InlineFocusRing extends StatefulWidget {
  const InlineFocusRing({super.key, required this.child});

  final Widget child;

  @override
  State<InlineFocusRing> createState() => _InlineFocusRingState();
}

class _InlineFocusRingState extends State<InlineFocusRing> {
  bool _focused = false;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final colors = Theme.of(context).colorScheme;
    return Focus(
      canRequestFocus: false,
      skipTraversal: true,
      includeSemantics: false,
      onFocusChange: (value) => setState(() => _focused = value),
      child: DecoratedBox(
        position: DecorationPosition.foreground,
        decoration: BoxDecoration(
          borderRadius: BorderRadius.circular(2),
          border: Border.all(
            color: _focused ? colors.onSurface : Colors.transparent,
            width: 2,
            strokeAlign: BorderSide.strokeAlignOutside,
          ),
        ),
        child: widget.child,
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// The link's text. A `WidgetSpan` scales its widget by the text size
/// setting as a whole, so text inside it that scales as well is scaled
/// twice: at 200% it would be four times its size
/// (docs/flutter-limitations.md, FL-241). `withNoTextScaling` leaves the
/// scaling to the `WidgetSpan`, and the link stays the size of the words
/// round it.
Widget linkText(InlineLink link, TextStyle style) =>
    MediaQuery.withNoTextScaling(child: Text(link.text, style: style));

/// A Material link in a sentence: `Semantics(link: true, linkUrl:)` round an
/// `InkWell`, as C03's `good-material` without its minimum height. A link
/// inside a sentence is exempt from the target size minimum (2.5.8).
class MaterialInlineLink extends StatelessWidget {
  const MaterialInlineLink({
    super.key,
    required this.link,
    required this.style,
  });

  final InlineLink link;
  final TextStyle style;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    return Semantics(
      link: true,
      linkUrl: link.address,
      onTapHint: linkTapHint,
      child: BrowserKeys(
        child: InlineFocusRing(
          child: InkWell(
            onTap: () => followLink(context, link),
            child: linkText(link, style),
          ),
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// A link in a sentence from the widgets layer: `FocusableActionDetector`
/// gives keyboard focus and, off the web, Enter and Space; a
/// `GestureDetector` takes the taps. On the web it registers no key action,
/// so the keys go to the browser, which follows the link on Enter.
class KeyboardInlineLink extends StatelessWidget {
  const KeyboardInlineLink({
    super.key,
    required this.link,
    required this.style,
  });

  final InlineLink link;
  final TextStyle style;

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    void follow() => followLink(context, link);
    return InlineFocusRing(
      child: FocusableActionDetector(
        mouseCursor: SystemMouseCursors.click,
        actions: kIsWeb
            ? const <Type, Action<Intent>>{}
            : {
                ActivateIntent: CallbackAction<ActivateIntent>(
                  onInvoke: (_) => follow(),
                ),
                ButtonActivateIntent: CallbackAction<ButtonActivateIntent>(
                  onInvoke: (_) => follow(),
                ),
              },
        child: Semantics(
          link: true,
          linkUrl: link.address,
          onTapHint: linkTapHint,
          child: GestureDetector(
            behavior: HitTestBehavior.opaque,
            onTap: follow,
            child: linkText(link, style),
          ),
        ),
      ),
    );
  }
}

/// A sentence of words and links: "Read about [links in text] and
/// [stand-alone links] first." Each link is a `WidgetSpan`, so that it can
/// take keyboard focus, which a `TextSpan` cannot (`bad-text-span`).
///
/// With [inOrder], the two parts of FL-413's workaround keep each link in
/// its place for screen readers (docs/flutter-limitations.md, FL-413):
///
/// - a `semanticsIdentifier` on the first words, which makes the paragraph
///   give each run of words a node of its own, with the sort keys 0, 1, 2,
///   and so on, in place of one label with no links in it;
/// - a sort key for each link between those of the runs round it: a link
///   after the first run is 0.5, after the second 1.5. Each run here is one
///   `TextSpan`. A span with an identifier is a run of its own, so a second
///   span after it would be a second run (docs/evidence/probes/c04).
///
/// Each link's node has the identifier `c04-<slug>-link-<n>`, from 1, put on
/// the container that has the sort key; `TestId` would drop the sort key.
class LinkSentence extends StatelessWidget {
  const LinkSentence({
    super.key,
    required this.slug,
    required this.linkBuilder,
    this.links = const [inTextLink, standAloneLink],
    this.inOrder = true,
  });

  final String slug;
  final List<InlineLink> links;
  final Widget Function(InlineLink link, TextStyle style) linkBuilder;
  final bool inOrder;

  /// The words before, between, and after the links.
  List<String> get words => links.length == 1
      ? const ['Read about ', ' first.']
      : const ['Read about ', ' and ', ' first.'];

  @override
  Widget build(BuildContext context) {
    final style = Theme.of(context).textTheme.bodyLarge!;
    final spans = <InlineSpan>[
      TextSpan(
        text: words.first,
        semanticsIdentifier: inOrder ? 'c04-$slug-sentence' : null,
      ),
    ];
    for (var i = 0; i < links.length; i++) {
      spans
        ..add(
          WidgetSpan(
            alignment: PlaceholderAlignment.baseline,
            baseline: TextBaseline.alphabetic,
            child: Semantics(
              key: ValueKey<String>('c04-$slug-link-${i + 1}'),
              container: true,
              identifier: 'c04-$slug-link-${i + 1}',
              // Between run i (key i) and run i + 1.
              sortKey: inOrder ? OrdinalSortKey(i + 0.5) : null,
              child: linkBuilder(links[i], style),
            ),
          ),
        )
        ..add(TextSpan(text: words[i + 1]));
    }
    // The links' text takes this style too; a WidgetSpan's widget does not
    // inherit the sentence's spans.
    return DefaultTextStyle.merge(
      style: style,
      child: Text.rich(TextSpan(children: spans)),
    );
  }
}
```

## What each platform exposes

Measured on Flutter 3.47.5. Screen reader speech is from the transcripts
below; the rest from the tree tests and the probe
([evidence/probes/c04](../evidence/probes/c04/README.md)).

| | Web | Android | iOS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A good sentence | the words as text and each link an `<a href>`, in the sentence's order, inside one element | one node holding the words' nodes and the links' nodes in order; each link a `View` with its address in a `URLSpan` | the words as text and each link a link, read in order |
| Screen reader on a link | "link links in text" | "links in text / Double-tap to open link" | "links in text Link" |
| Tab | Back, the first link, the second link | the same | the same (Tab, Tab, Enter follows the first) |
| A `TextSpan` link (`bad-text-span`) | an `<a>` with no `href`, skipped by Tab | a `Button`, skipped by Tab | a link; no key reached it |
| `WidgetSpan` links with no workaround | a group named by the words, the links inside it | the words' node, then the links | the words, then the links |
| The sentence merged | one `<a href>` with the first link's address | one `View`, one `URLSpan` | one link |

Things to know:

- **Flutter's inline link is not reachable by keyboard.** A `TextSpan`
  with a recognizer is the SDK's way, and it is read well, but no key
  reaches it on any platform ([FL-240](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-240)).
  The reference repositories found the same on iOS for SwiftUI's own inline
  links.
- **A `WidgetSpan` link is a box in the line.** It cannot break across
  lines with the words: a link that does not fit what is left of the line
  goes to the next, and a long one wraps inside its own box
  ([FL-244](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-244)). A `TextSpan` link flows
  with the text. Keep link text short, as these examples do.
- **Android and iOS read a Flutter sentence differently from their own
  text views.** A native Android `TextView` with links is one stop for
  TalkBack, which offers its links in a menu; the
  [Android View](https://github.com/cvs-health/android-view-accessibility-techniques/blob/main/doc/componenttypes/LinksInlineWithText.md)
  and Android Compose references build it that way. A Flutter sentence is a stop per run of
  words and per link, and has no such menu. Merging it into one stop, as the
  Android Compose reference does for its own text, loses the second link
  in Flutter (`bad-merged`).
- **On iOS, a VoiceOver touch on a link can find the words after it.** A
  run of words that wraps has one node over the whole of its lines; in the
  good examples on the iPhone 17 screen, " first." lies over both links,
  and a tap at a link with VoiceOver on lands on "first." Swiping reaches
  each link ([FL-246](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-246)).
- **`TestId` cannot go round the sentence.** Its `MergeSemantics` makes
  `bad-merged`. The identifiers sit on each link's own container.
- **The colour is checked by the widget tests only.** The web, Android, and
  iOS trees do not carry a link's colour or underline.

## Inline links in the doc renderer

The app's doc renderer (`lib/app/doc_view.dart`) shows a link inside a
paragraph as plain words and lists the link as a stand-alone link under the
paragraph, because "a link inside running text is not reliably reachable on
every platform". The answer this technique gives: it can be, with the SDK,
but not with the renderer's `TextSpan`s. Not changed here, as the renderer
is shared; what it would take:

1. Build each `a` as a `WidgetSpan` holding the shell's link (the
   `ExternalLink` of `lib/app/widgets.dart`, without its icon and its
   48 pixel height, which a link in a sentence does not need), with the
   link's text in `MediaQuery.withNoTextScaling` and its style taken from
   the paragraph (a `WidgetSpan`'s widget does not inherit the spans'
   style).
2. Give the paragraph's first span a `semanticsIdentifier`, and each link a
   `Semantics(container: true, sortKey: OrdinalSortKey(k - 0.5))`, where
   `k` is the number of runs of words before it. The count is the
   paragraph's, not the Markdown's: the span with the identifier is a run
   of its own, and the spans after it up to the next link make one run,
   whatever their style. So "Read **about** [link]" with the identifier on
   "Read " has two runs before the link, and its key is 1.5, not 0.5 (the
   probe's "two spans" lines; framework source: `combineSemanticsInfo`).
   Two links with no words between them need two keys between the same
   runs (not tested).
3. Keep the renderer's own `MergeSemantics` and `TestId` off the
   paragraph.
4. Accept that a long link moves to a line of its own and wraps inside its
   own box (FL-244); Markdown links in the docs are often long titles.

## Flutter limitations

Found on Flutter 3.47.5. Full detail is in the
[limitations log](../flutter-limitations.md).

- [FL-240](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-240): a link in a `TextSpan` takes no keyboard focus and cannot have an address.
- [FL-241](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-241): text in a `WidgetSpan` is scaled twice by the text size setting.
- [FL-242](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-242): `CupertinoColors.link` and `systemBlue` are under 4.5:1 on white.
- [FL-243](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-243): `MergeSemantics` round a sentence with links makes one link, with the first link's address and action.
- [FL-244](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-244): a `WidgetSpan` link cannot break across lines with the text.
- [FL-245](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-245): on the web `SelectableText` is an empty, disabled text field, and its links are gone.
- [FL-246](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-246): a run of text that wraps has one node over all its lines; on iOS a VoiceOver touch on a link can find the words.
- [FL-413](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-413): the text round a `WidgetSpan` is one node that leaves the widget out.
- [FL-220](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-220): on Android a Flutter link says no role.
- [FL-032](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-032): a link with no address is a button on Android and has no `href` on the web.
- [FL-013](../flutter-limitations.md#fl-013): on the web the browser follows a link's `href` as well as the app's `onTap`.

The VoiceOver tests compare the whole list of what is spoken after the
"Example" heading, so they also show the order and what is not spoken.
TalkBack's walk moves focus to each part of the sentence in turn, so it
shows what is said for each part and not where a swipe goes; the tree test
checks the parts' order.

Not yet covered: which elements a TalkBack swipe stops on (the walk moves
focus by action; the probe's swipes sent through UI Automator did not move
TalkBack's focus); TalkBack's links menu, which a Flutter sentence does not
offer; links out of the app, which the SDK cannot open off the web; a
link's visited state; a link's hover cue; right-to-left text; and a
sentence with a link at its start or two links with no words between
them, which need sort keys the examples do not use.
